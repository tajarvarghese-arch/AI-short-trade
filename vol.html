So is it expensive?

  • Not by level. SMH 30-day implied vol is in the 25th percentile of its 52-week range and NVDA is in the 1st. Both trade below their own realised vol. VXN is in the 35th percentile of its year. You are buying vol cheaper than it has been for most of the past twelve months.
  • Yes by skew, and that is normal. The 20% out-of-the-money strike carries 5 to 6 vol points more than at the money on both names. That is what makes a 430 SMH put cost 1.3% of spot for 107 days. Skew is the price of the crash you are trying to own, and it is not unusually steep: SKEW is near its 1-year median.
  • The feel of expense comes from the payoff shape. A 20% out-of-the-money put needs the complex down more than 21% before it pays a cent at expiry. That is why the desk favors spreads, which sell the even richer deeper strike back. The cost of the position is small; the probability it expires worthless is high. Those are different things.
  • What would change the answer. If SMH implied vol were above 45% or VXN above 28, this page would say wait. Longer tenors are the better buy right now: the term structure is flat at the 20% strike, so June covers three times as many days as October for about 2.4 times the premium on SMH.
